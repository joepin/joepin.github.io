A period of small steps

Four independent games for sixth-grade Tanach learners who need explicit instruction and repeated practice. All practice is drawn from Devarim 16–18. Students choose one skill for the period.

GameSkill gapWhat students do
Root GardenCannot locate a shoresh in a longer wordChoose roots, build them with Hebrew letter tiles, connect roots to meanings
Word WorkshopMisses attached prefixes and suffixesDecode small parts, then build the word’s English meaning
Verb DetectiveConfuses the subject with the receiver or mixes up singular and pluralIdentify subjects, compare forms, choose a contextual meaning
Pasuk PathRecognizes isolated words but cannot combine their meaningChoose accurate meanings and arrange short English phrase tiles

Suggested 40-minute flow

5 minLearn & try
10 minGuided round
10 minIndependent round
10 minSkill challenge
5 minCorrections

The pacing is approximate. Every game has 36 core tasks, a short lesson and worked examples, a lesson check, a correction round, and additional 12-question practice rounds. Stronger students can continue practicing. Students who need more time can pause and resume. The timer is a guide and never ends the activity or penalizes slower reading.

What to tell the class

“Choose the skill that would help you most today. Read the mini-lesson and each explanation. Use hints whenever you need them. Work in the same game for the period, correct your tricky questions, and look at your progress. You may retry as often as you need.”

Feedback and scores

Every submitted answer receives immediate feedback and a specific explanation. After an incorrect answer, the student tries the task again before continuing. The initial score counts only the first submission on each of the 36 core tasks. A separate measure shows first-try answers without requested hints. Automatic teaching tips in the guided round are part of instruction. Corrections and extra practice do not inflate the initial score. All initially missed or hinted tasks return in the correction round; a student with none receives six confidence checks. The lesson check is unscored.

The three core rounds use increasing independence and reshuffled tasks and choices. Retakes may include familiar material: repetition is deliberate. Vocabulary is limited to these three perakim. This activity practices reading skills; it does not teach the full halachic scope of a pasuk.

Sources and provenance

Only the biblical text and its plain translation are used. No Rashi or other commentaries are used in the lessons, hints, answers, or explanations. Source identity was checked against the institutions’ own descriptions. All game instructions, exercises, and explanations are original classroom wording; they are not quotations attributed to a rabbi.

Hebrew is displayed with nekudot and without cantillation marks. Practice usually uses excerpts rather than full pesukim. Grammar labels describe the forms in these pesukim; patterns are taught as clues, not universal rules. Hebrew final letter forms count as the same underlying letters. Student access and privacy

No names, accounts, tracking services, or teacher dashboard. Results and unfinished games remain only in the current browser on the current device when browser storage is available. They do not transfer between devices. A shared device should use “New student: clear progress” before a different student begins. Students can print their own results if desired.

Putting it on your school website

This is a complete, self-contained HTML website with no outside scripts, fonts, or required internet services. To host it, give this file to the person who manages your school website. It can be named index.html and uploaded to any static website host that runs JavaScript. Share the resulting website URL with students. Uploading a file to a document viewer alone may not run the games. Students can also download the HTML file and open it directly in a modern browser.

Public hosting has not been activated by this file. Test the resulting school URL on a student computer before class.
